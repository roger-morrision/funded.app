# Fee router v3: 5% buyback enforcement

The checked-in v2 `settle_mint` instruction checks router authority and records a transfer. It does not bind the transfer to the launch's category allocations. Changing the JavaScript policy does not upgrade a deployed program or reserve SOL in a contract-controlled buyback vault.

## Required versioned contract change

1. Initialize a new per-mint router PDA and immutable policy account for launches that opt into v3. Store the mint, router authority, and six configurable protocol rates in ten-millionths of gross fees (defaults: operations `1000000`, referral levels `200000 / 60000 / 40000`, community `200000`, buyback `500000`), with the creator-directed `8000000` fixed. This precision supports the four decimal places accepted by the application configuration. Reject policies whose six rates do not total `2000000`. Keep the v2 PDA and policy hash for existing launches.
2. Claim Pump creator fees into the v3 router PDA. A v3 settlement must consume a unique claim ID once, calculate all five obligations from the **confirmed amount actually claimed**, and verify the destination account for each category. The 500 basis-point buyback amount must move to the buyback vault in the same successful settlement transaction. Deterministic integer rounding must account for every lamport without exceeding the claim amount; include dust cases in tests.
3. The buyback vault must have no general withdrawal instruction. Only a separate, audited execution path may spend its balance on a verified $FUNDED market route, then burn the acquired $FUNDED using SPL `BurnChecked`. A reservation or swap is not itself a burn. Publish claim, buy, burn, and supply-delta receipts separately.
4. Migrate the API and launch client to the v3 PDA and instruction only after the contract is built, audited, deployed on Devnet, and exercised with finalized claims and payout balance deltas. Existing v2 launches keep their saved fee policies and settlement path.

## Acceptance checks before activation

- A 100 SOL finalized claim produces 80 SOL creator-directed, 10 SOL operations, 3 SOL referral obligations, 2 SOL community, and 5 SOL in the contract-controlled buyback vault.
- A caller cannot redirect the buyback transfer, lower its amount, substitute the mint, replay a claim ID, or withdraw buyback SOL as an operations payout.
- A one-lamport claim and other rounding boundaries settle exactly once with no lost or created lamports.
- A legacy v2 launch still calculates from its saved launch policy and does not silently change to 5%.
- A burn is displayed as confirmed only after its finalized `BurnChecked` transaction and mint supply decrease have been verified.

Until these checks pass, the website's 5% figure describes the new **application allocation policy**, not an on-chain-enforced buyback or a completed burn.

## Current implementation status

The isolated v3 code initializes a separate mint router and immutable policy, then atomically sweeps its available balance into seven program-derived category vaults using cumulative rounding. The six protocol rates are set at initialization from the configuration; the creator rate is fixed at 80%. Initialization requires creator and mint signatures and reads the current global authority from the on-chain router header without requiring that authority to co-sign the launch. The policy records that initial authority, while settlement requires the current global router authority so a legitimate rotation does not strand funds. Settlement rejects a router balance that differs from the exact expected collection amount; a synthetic finalized-transaction verifier checks the Pump V2 collection instruction and router credit before the client constructs that amount and claim ID. A separate disbursement instruction requires the current authority and creator signatures, records one-time receipts, pays the creator category only to the creator wallet, pays the community category only into its existing proof-gated reward vault, and cannot spend or redirect the buyback category. Operations and referral releases require both signatures and an ordinary system wallet recipient; this does not establish referral eligibility. LiteSVM tests exercise exact distribution, forged initial authority, wrong collection amount, redirected vault and mint substitution, replay, authority rotation, one-lamport rounding, allowed releases, and blocked cross-category and buyback releases. Existing v2 code and accounts are unchanged.

This is not an activated payout path. The v3 program enforces the supplied balance amount but cannot itself authenticate the off-chain transaction proof or prove that a particular Pump claim produced those lamports. A server-side collection loader now fetches the transaction at finalized commitment, re-verifies the Pump launch creator, and checks the router and immutable policy accounts against the recorded mint and protocol split. Its tests use synthetic RPC and launch fixtures. No v3 settlement route calls this loader yet, and no real v3 collection has been verified through it. Operations and referral disbursements do not verify the ultimate recipient's entitlement, and moving community SOL into a reward vault is not a completed recipient payout. The buyback vault has no swap-and-burn instruction, and no Devnet v3 program or launch client has been deployed. These pieces, an external contract review, and finalized Devnet transactions remain required before the app can claim contract-enforced end-to-end payouts or burn completion.
